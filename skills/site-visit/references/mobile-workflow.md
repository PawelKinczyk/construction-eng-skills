# Working from a phone on site

This skill runs with no scripts, no files and no Python. Everything is the agent reading photos and text, so the limits below are about input quality and about how much a phone session can hold, not about software.

## 1. What works well on a phone

| Task | How |
|---|---|
| Dictate the visit | in the car or in the lift, one continuous dictation: what was seen, by area, plus anything agreed. Structure is added by the agent |
| Read photos | separate batches by area or by level; add one line per batch ("skan B, poziom 0, izolacja i zawory") |
| Quick report | summary, observations, safety, follow-up list - sendable in minutes |
| Ask questions | "is this a defect or a design question?", "who did I say owns this?" |
| Cross-check your own report | paste the report back and ask what is missing against the structure in `site-report-format.md` |

## 2. What to leave for the desktop

- `.docx` in the project template, with the letterhead and the signature block.
- Attaching drawings, photos in a numbered folder, or a photo-contact-sheet file.
- Numbering the report from the project register, entering it in the correspondence register (`project-correspondence`), and linking it to the visit's follow-up items.
- Adding test certificates, delivery notes and other documents collected on site.
- Building the punch list register file from the defects in the report (`punch-list`).

## 3. Dictation that produces a good report

Say the area before the observation; every time. ("Skrzydło A, poziom 0, przy pionie W2: izolacja kończy się 300 mm przed zaworem.") A stream of observations with no place markers produces a report that cannot be checked.

Include, without being asked: date and time, weather, who was there, what was agreed, and who was told about a safety issue.

Say the numbers when they matter, and say whether they were measured or estimated: "około 300 mm, zmierzone taśmą".

## 4. Photo practice

- Shoot both the wide view and the close-up for anything that may become a defect item. The wide view places it; the close-up shows it.
- Put something with a known size in the frame for a defect (a tape, a boot, a hand).
- Phone photos carry the date and the time in the file metadata; do not retype them, but say the area for each batch so the agent can label them.
- Do not delete a bad photo on site. Deleting evidence is a habit that costs projects.
- Where a photo came from someone else (a chat, another site, an earlier date), say so. It is not evidence of this visit.

## 5. No signal and long visits

- Dictate into a voice memo app and process it later: the voice note plus the photos is enough for the full report.
- Keep the notes in the order you walked the site. That order is the natural structure of the observations.
- For a long visit, produce one report per day or per area rather than one report for a week; a report written four days later is worth much less.
- Write down the time when something happened that may matter later (a safety incident, an instruction, a stop): time is what turns an observation into a record.

## 6. Tone on the phone

Ask for terse answers when you are on site ("obs", "idź dalej", "skróć"). The agent keeps the report structure in the background; the style rules in `site-report-format.md` apply to the report itself, not to the conversation.

## 7. Privacy and data

- Site photos often contain people. Do not name individuals beyond their role on the project, and do not describe people who are not part of the record.
- Photos of persons for a safety or incident record are attached only when the purpose requires it, and the report says who asked for them.
- Names, roles and companies are the limit. Phone numbers and personal details do not go into a report that is distributed.
