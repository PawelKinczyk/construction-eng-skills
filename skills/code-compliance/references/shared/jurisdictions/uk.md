## Legal hierarchy

England (and, where noted, Wales):

1. **Building Act 1984** (primary Act). Parts: I Building Regulations; II supervision of building work otherwise than by building control authorities; 2A regulation of the building control profession; III other provisions about buildings. Read on legislation.gov.uk. Its extent is not shown on the page read: to verify.
2. **Building Regulations 2010 (SI 2010/2214)**. Read: Parts 1 to 10 (including Part 2A dutyholders and competence, Part 2B Wales dutyholders and competence, Part 7A automatic fire suppression, Part 9A electronic communications, Part 9B electric vehicle charging). Schedule 1 holds the technical requirements. Schedule 2 lists exempt buildings and work. Schedule 3 lists self-certification schemes. Transitional regulations 49 to 53 cover the 2010 change-over only.
3. **Approved Documents** (statutory guidance, England). Following them is a normal way to show compliance, but they are not the only way. The status wording was not on the pages read: to verify exact wording before quoting it.
4. **British Standards and other guidance** referenced by the Approved Documents. These are not law. The Approved Document makes them relevant.
5. **Building Safety Act 2022**. Read: Part 2 the Building Safety Regulator, Part 3 changes to the Building Act 1984, Part 4 higher-risk buildings, Part 5 other provision about safety and standards. It adds the higher-risk building regime and amends the Building Act 1984.
6. **Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015 (SI 2015/51)**. Health and safety law, separate from the Building Regulations. Applies in England, Wales and Scotland (extent to verify).

Conflict rule: an Act beats regulations. Regulations beat guidance. An Approved Document does not change the regulations. If a standard conflicts with the Building Regulations, the Regulations win.

## Higher-risk buildings (England)

Read on legislation.gov.uk and gov.uk:
- Building Safety Act 2022 section 65 defines a higher-risk building (occupation phase) as a building in England at least 18 metres in height or with at least 7 storeys, with at least 2 residential units. Check the current consolidated text: legislation.gov.uk shows changes not yet applied.
- Building Act 1984 section 120D, as inserted by the 2022 Act, sets the design and construction phase definition (at least 18 metres or 7 storeys, plus a description in regulations). The Higher-Risk Buildings (Descriptions and Supplementary Provisions) Regulations 2023 supplement both definitions: they define "building" and "storey", set how height is measured, and exclude some buildings. Per official guidance in search results, hotels, secure residential institutions and military premises are excluded from both; hospitals and care homes are excluded from the occupation part only. Detailed rules: to verify on the instrument.
- gov.uk "Building regulations approval" says higher-risk buildings are at least 7 storeys or 18 metres and also meet one of: 2 residential units, a hospital, or a care home (design and construction phase wording). It also says BSR approval is needed before starting a new higher-risk building, and before work that makes an existing building higher-risk or stops it being higher-risk.
- Gateways: Gateway 2 is Building Safety Regulator building control approval before relevant building work starts. Gateway 3 is completion: the Regulator must be satisfied that the as-built building complies. Gateway 1 (planning stage) is a planning matter: to verify.
- For higher-risk building work, the Building Safety Regulator is the building control authority. Local authorities and registered building control approvers do not approve it.
- The legislation.gov.uk Higher-Risk Buildings (Keeping and Provision of Information etc.) (England) Regulations 2024 exist (SI 2024/41). Content: to verify.

Wales: the higher-risk building regime in the 2022 Act is defined for buildings in England. Welsh position for dutyholders is Part 2B of the Building Regulations 2010. Anything else for Wales: to verify.

## Permits and approvals

### England: building control routes (read on gov.uk)

- For work that is not a higher-risk building, the applicant chooses either the local authority building control department or a private registered building control approver.
- Higher-risk buildings: approval from the Building Safety Regulator is needed before building work starts.
- Registered building control approvers (RBCAs) replaced approved inspectors. Per official pages: businesses could register with the Building Safety Regulator from 10 October 2023, and RBCA registration replaced approved inspector approval from 6 April 2024. Registration lasts five years (to verify on the guidance page). The approver gives an initial notice to the local authority.
- Approved inspector transition: an initial notice given by an approved inspector who did not register could not be continued for higher-risk building work after 6 April 2024. For other work such a notice stayed valid until 1 October 2024. This is past. The instruments are SI 2023/906 and SI 2024/110 on legislation.gov.uk: to verify details before relying on them.
- Application types (local authority route, gov.uk search results): full plans; building notice; regularisation of work done without approval (local authority only). A completion certificate is issued at the end. Statutory timings (for example the 5 week decision period for full plans) came from search results, not the regulation: to verify in the Building Regulations 2010 (reg. 14 full plans, reg. 12 and 13 building notice, reg. 17 completion certificate: numbers read from the contents page).
- Competent person schemes: work done by a registered scheme member does not need separate approval (read on gov.uk).
- Dutyholders: Part 2A of the Building Regulations 2010 applies from 1 October 2023 (insertion date read on legislation.gov.uk; made by SI 2023/911). It sets client, principal designer, principal contractor, designer and contractor duties and competence for all building work in England. Regulation numbers beyond the Part 2A heading: to verify.
- A gov.uk consultation proposes full plans only for new dwellings under the council route, tied to 1 October 2026. Outcome not confirmed: to verify. gov.uk also says from 1 October 2026 some applications for certain residential buildings may be subject to the Building Safety Levy: to verify.

### Wales

Building Regulations 2010 applied in Wales with Welsh Approved Documents. Building control by local authority or approved inspector route: Welsh detail to verify (RBCA registration is in the England regulations; the Welsh position was not confirmed).

### Scotland (read on gov.scot)

- A **building warrant** (permission to build) and a **completion certificate** (permission to occupy) are granted by a local authority **verifier**. Applications go through the eBuildingStandards portal.
- The Technical Handbooks (domestic and non-domestic) hold the standards. The edition depends on the date the building warrant application is submitted, or the date work that needs no warrant starts.
- The April 2026 domestic and non-domestic handbooks apply to warrant applications submitted on or after 6 April 2026 (per gov.scot search results; transitional detail is in clause 0.2.1 of Section 0 in the handbook: to verify). The January 2025 editions are the previous editions. gov.scot says full versions are expected in summer 2026: to verify whether they are out.
- Procedural handbook third edition, version 1.9 took effect 1 April 2026 (revised fee table).

### Northern Ireland (read on finance-ni.gov.uk search results)

The Building Regulations (Northern Ireland) 2012 took effect on 31 October 2012. Technical Booklets A to V carry the guidance, with later amending booklets. Compliance with a booklet gives a presumption of compliance. District councils operate building control: to verify on nidirect.gov.uk.

## Transition rules

The rule is the same shape in England for every change: the new edition does not apply to a project if a building notice, initial notice or full plans application was submitted before the commencement date, and work has started (or has started and is sufficiently progressed) within the stated period. Always read the transitional text in the current circular for each change. A drawing set dated after the commencement date may still follow the old edition. Ask the user for the notice or application date.

England (read):

| Change | Coming into force | Transition |
|---|---|---|
| Approved Document L (2021 edition with 2023 amendments), F, O, S | 15 June 2022 for L and the amending regulations | Old edition applies if notice or full plans were given before 15 June 2022 and work started before 15 June 2023 (read for L). Only buildings where work has started can use it on multi-building sites. |
| Approved Document B: care home sprinklers; withdrawal of BS 476 reaction to fire and roof national classes | 2 March 2025 | Previous edition applies if notice or application was before 2 March 2025 and work was sufficiently progressed before that day or within 6 months starting on it. |
| Approved Document B: withdrawal of BS 476 fire resistance national classes | 2 September 2029 | Same pattern, 6 months. |
| Approved Document B: second stairs in residential buildings over 18 m | 30 September 2026 | Subject to transitional provisions. The gov.uk table gives an 18 month period. The exact text is in the 2026 amendments booklet: to verify. |
| Approved Document B: evacuation lifts | not found on the page read | to verify |
| Future Homes and Buildings Standards: Building Regulations etc. (Amendment) (England) Regulations 2026 (SI 2026/335), new Approved Document L Volumes 1 and 2 and Approved Document F Volume 1 | 24 March 2027 (non-higher-risk work); 24 September 2027 for higher-risk building work and work to existing higher-risk buildings for regs 3, 4, 6, 7, 8 and 9 | Not applicable to a building where a building notice, initial notice or full plans were given before 24 March 2027, if building work starts before 24 March 2028. For higher-risk building work: not applicable if a valid approval application reached the Regulator before 24 September 2027 and was not rejected on or after that date. Read in Building Circular 01/2026. The regulations text on legislation.gov.uk was not read (dates not shown on page): to verify. |

"Sufficiently progressed" in Approved Document B transitional text: for new building, concrete pouring for the permanent foundations, or permanent piling, has started. For work to an existing building, the work has started (read).

Wales: Welsh Approved Documents have their own dates (read, the page does not say whether they are publication or effective dates): B 19 June 2025 (amendment slip applicable from 20 December 2025), F 7 April 2026, L 7 April 2026, G 10 May 2023, J 6 January 2025, K 19 March 2025, M 20 March 2025, O 19 March 2025, P 3 April 2017, R 2 July 2025, 7 20 December 2025. Scotland: edition set by the date of the building warrant application (see above).

## Units and conventions

- SI units. Drawings in millimetres, levels in metres.
- Dates: DD/MM/YYYY in documents; ISO 8601 for data and file names.
- Decimal point is a full stop. Thousands separator is a comma.
- Document language: English. Wales: Welsh and English documents may both exist for public bodies (to verify). Scotland and Northern Ireland: English.
- Drawing and information management conventions: BS 1192 was replaced by BS EN ISO 19650 series for information management (BIM), to verify the adoption status and edition. BS 8541 for BIM objects (to verify).
- Contract forms, names only: JCT (Standard Building Contract, Design and Build Contract, Minor Works), NEC (NEC4 Engineering and Construction Contract, Professional Services Contract), FIDIC for international work. Edition and terms: not checked.
- CDM 2015 (read): a project is notifiable to the Health and Safety Executive if construction work is scheduled to last longer than 30 working days with more than 20 workers at the same time, or to exceed 500 person days (Regulation 6). Regulation numbers read on legislation.gov.uk: 5 appointment of principal designer and principal contractor; 6 notification; 11 principal designer duties; 12 construction phase plan and health and safety file; 13 principal contractor duties. Unverified: the exact content of each regulation beyond the contents page.
