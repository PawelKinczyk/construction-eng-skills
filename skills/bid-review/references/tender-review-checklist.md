# Tender review checklist

Use this as the working list. It is the questions to answer from the documents, not a substitute for reading them. Every answer or unknown goes into the review report with the document and clause.

## 1. Document set

| # | Document | Received | Revision / date | What it decides |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Invitation / ITT / SWZ | | | rules of the tender, deadlines, criteria |
| 2 | Instructions to tenderers | | | submission format, questions deadline, validity |
| 3 | Corrigenda / amendments | | | changes to any of the above, and to scope |
| 4 | Technical description / PFU | | | what is to be built |
| 5 | Drawings and schedules | | | quantities, interfaces, buildability |
| 6 | Specification (STWiOR or equivalent) | | | materials, testing, workmanship, execution |
| 7 | BOQ / price form | | | pricing structure and quantities to price |
| 8 | Contract conditions (form + particular) | | | risk allocation, money, time, remedies |
| 9 | Payment and securities schedule | | | cashflow, guarantees |
| 10 | Insurance requirements | | | cost and availability |
| 11 | Programme / milestones | | | time risk, resources |
| 12 | Site information, surveys, GI report | | | ground and existing conditions |
| 13 | Permit and utility status | | | who obtains what, when |
| 14 | BIM / information requirements | | | model, data, deliverables |
| 15 | Evaluation criteria and scoring | | | how the offer is judged |
| 16 | Answer to questions / clarifications | | | changes the risk picture, sometimes late |

Rule: a review based on an incomplete set must say which documents were missing and which conclusions are therefore provisional.

## 2. Scope

- Is everything in the drawings, the specification and the BOQ consistent? List the differences.
- Which works are in another contract (enabling, demolition, fit-out, utilities, fit-out of the process)? Where is the interface?
- Which works does the client supply (free issue, material, equipment, design)?
- Is design included or is the design complete and only execution is priced? Check the responsibility for the design.
- Are quantities to be verified by the bidder (re-measure) or are they guaranteed by the client (lump sum, no re-measure)? This changes the quantity risk completely.
- Any works described in words but absent from the BOQ, or in the BOQ but absent from drawings?
- Site establishment, temporary works, site services, hoardings, welfare, cleaning, security, snow clearance: where are they priced? Preliminaries are a common omission in comparison.
- Testing, commissioning, training, spare parts, as-built documents, digital deliverables: priced or not?

## 3. Contract conditions

Review with the actual contract document open. This list is the set of questions.

Time and programme
- Commencement, possession, completion and milestone dates. Are milestones tied to liquidated damages?
- Extension of time: which grounds are listed, and are they closed or open?
- Force majeure: definition, notice, consequences, and the position on epidemics, strikes and supply failures.
- Programme submission and acceptance obligations, and float ownership.

Money
- Payment terms: intervals, certification, payment period, and who certifies.
- Advance payment and its guarantee; retention percentage, cap and release.
- Price adjustment: fixed price, indexation clause, currency clause, and the trigger for a variation.
- Variation mechanics: who instructs, how it is valued (rates in the contract, then market rates, then cost plus), and the treatment of preliminaries and overheads in the valuation.
- Provisional sums, dayworks and their adjustment rules.

Guarantees and liability
- Performance security, retention bond, parent company guarantee: values, validity, conditions for calling.
- Liquidated damages: rate, cap, and whether partial possession or sectional completion reduces them.
- Liability cap: total, and whether it excludes consequential loss.
- Warranty and defects liability: duration per element, and the response times.
- Indemnities and the insurance requirements (CAR, third party, professional indemnity, workmen's compensation) with limits and deductibles.

Risk allocation
- Ground conditions: who owns the risk (unforeseeable physical conditions clause, or a waiver)?
- Existing structures, contamination, archaeology, unexploded ordnance.
- Interface with the client's other contracts and with the operating building.
- Suspension and termination: grounds, notice, payment on termination, and the consequence of termination for convenience.
- Dispute resolution: engineer's decision, DAB, arbitration or court, and the seat and language.

Notices
- Notice periods and their strictness. A missed notice can lose a valid claim.
- Form of notice and who may issue it.

## 4. Commercial and market

- Who else is likely to bid, and on what basis (local presence, own labour, own resources)?
- Is the client known for change orders, claims or aggressive valuation? Use your own project history, not gossip.
- Local content, subcontracting restrictions, or a nominated subcontractor list.
- Currency exposure, tax regime (VAT, reverse charge, withholding), customs and import duty on equipment, and local labour rules.
- Working hours, holidays, restrictions, labour availability and rates for the locality and the season.

## 5. Red flags

- The tender closes very soon after issue, with a complex scope.
- The documents contradict each other and no corrigendum answers it.
- Quantities materially below what the drawings show, with a re-measure clause that turns it into a claim fight later.
- Payment terms outside the usual market practice for that country, or an unfunded project.
- A very low or absent liquidated damages figure combined with a heavy programme penalty elsewhere.
- The scope arriving by reference only ("as per the standard practice of the client") without a document.
- A required warranty or guarantee beyond the market norm, or beyond the manufacturer's own warranty.
- Programme impossibility on the face of it (permits, deliveries, seasons).
- Insurance limits above what insurers will provide for the price assumed.
- The client refuses written answers to questions already asked in writing.

## 6. Review quality check

- Every risk has a document reference.
- Every question is answerable in writing and has a "why it matters".
- Deviations are written as proposed alternative wording, not as complaints.
- The price build-up separates cost, risk allowance, overhead and margin.
- The submission checklist is verified against the instructions, item by item, before the deadline.
